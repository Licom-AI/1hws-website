Mathematics AI questions, answered

How can Mathematics students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for Mathematics students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as explain a proof technique and define a group homomorphism intuitively through to project-scale work like design a numerical experiment. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a Mathematics major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For Mathematics that means explain a proof technique, define a group homomorphism intuitively and check a derivative or integral. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes build a study guide of definitions, theorems, and proof sketches and debug a proof draft.

What do I need to start using AI as a Mathematics student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 Mathematics use cases here draw on 13 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for Mathematics coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for Mathematics coursework at HWS?

Your Mathematics professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Design a numerical experiment" exists to get you through Mathematics material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
