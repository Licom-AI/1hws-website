History AI questions, answered

How can History students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for History students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as summarize a primary source and explain a historiographical term through to project-scale work like map spatial history with gis reasoning. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a History major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For History that means summarize a primary source, explain a historiographical term and brainstorm paper topics on reconstruction-era labor history. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes synthesize articles into a historiographical essay and build a document analysis framework.

What do I need to start using AI as a History student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 History use cases here draw on 17 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for History coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for History coursework at HWS?

Your History professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Map spatial history with GIS reasoning" exists to get you through History material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
