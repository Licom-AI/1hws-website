How can International Relations students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for International Relations students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as summarize a policy brief and explain realism versus liberalism in plain terms through to project-scale work like build a custom geopolitics-analysis gpt. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a International Relations major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For International Relations that means summarize a policy brief, explain realism versus liberalism in plain terms and brainstorm essay topics on diplomacy. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes synthesize multiple news sources and analyze trade or aid data.

What do I need to start using AI as a International Relations student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 International Relations use cases here draw on 15 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for International Relations coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for International Relations coursework at HWS?

Your International Relations professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Build a custom geopolitics-analysis GPT" exists to get you through International Relations material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
