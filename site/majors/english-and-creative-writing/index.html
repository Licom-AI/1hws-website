The linked tutorial covers ChatGPT's Study Mode (type /study), an interactive tutor that guides you with questions step by step instead of just handing over the answer, and the starter prompt below already frames it for English and Creative Writing.

How can English and Creative Writing students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for English and Creative Writing students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as define literary terms and summarize a dense novel chapter through to project-scale work like debug notebooklm source synthesis. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a English and Creative Writing major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For English and Creative Writing that means define literary terms, summarize a dense novel chapter and brainstorm short story premises. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes compare two authors' styles and build an argument map for a postcolonial research paper.

What do I need to start using AI as a English and Creative Writing student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 English and Creative Writing use cases here draw on 15 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for English and Creative Writing coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for English and Creative Writing coursework at HWS?

Your English and Creative Writing professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work.
