AI Use Cases for English and Creative Writing at HWS

Learn more about the English and Creative Writing program ↗

20 practical, difficulty-rated ways English and Creative Writing students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges can use AI — each naming the exact tutorial it links to and what you’ll take away. Click any card to watch it.

Before you use these on graded work: check your professor’s policy on AI. It differs by course, and these examples are study aids — not permission.

Easy

Define literary terms

Explain metonymy, free indirect discourse, or iambic pentameter with clear examples. The linked tutorial covers ChatGPT's Study Mode (type /study), an interactive tutor that guides you with questions step by step instead of just handing over the answer.

What you'll take awayHow to switch ChatGPT into tutor mode so it drills you on terms and ideas rather than doing the thinking for you.

Starter prompt — paste into ChatGPT
I'm an English and Creative Writing student at HWS. My task: Define literary terms — Explain metonymy, free indirect discourse, or iambic pentameter with clear examples.

Define each term in one clear sentence, then give a concrete example of each in context. Once I've read them, quiz me on them one at a time and tell me what I got wrong and why.

Summarize a dense novel chapter

Condense assigned Faulkner or Woolf reading into a plot recap. The linked tutorial covers MacMost's copy-paste method — pull an article into reader view, paste it into ChatGPT after 'summarize this:', then ask for a set word count or a 10-bullet version; also how to grab a YouTube transcript to summarise a video.

What you'll take awayHow to compress a long reading or video into a summary of the exact length you need, and why pasting the source keeps it accurate.

Starter prompt — paste into ChatGPT
I'm an English and Creative Writing student at HWS. My task: Summarize a dense novel chapter — Condense assigned Faulkner or Woolf reading into a plot recap.

Give me (1) a three-sentence summary, (2) five bullet-point key claims, and (3) anything the author asserts without supporting evidence. Text below:

Easy

Draft an email to a professor

Request a deadline extension on a portfolio submission politely. The linked tutorial covers drafting an email in ChatGPT and improving it through conversation — the first draft is deliberately generic, then detail is added turn by turn — plus what not to paste in for privacy.

What you'll take awayWhy the first draft always reads like AI, how to iterate it into your own voice, and to keep real names out of the prompt.

Starter prompt — paste into ChatGPT
I'm an English and Creative Writing student at HWS. My task: Draft an email to a professor — Request a deadline extension on a portfolio submission politely.

Keep it under 150 words: polite, specific, with one clear ask. Give me two versions — one formal, one warmer. Don't include real names, ID numbers, or anything private.
