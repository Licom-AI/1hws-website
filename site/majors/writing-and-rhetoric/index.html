How can Writing and Rhetoric students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for Writing and Rhetoric students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as brainstorm angles for a persuasive or personal essay and define rhetorical terms through to project-scale work like develop and test a style-transfer workflow. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a Writing and Rhetoric major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For Writing and Rhetoric that means brainstorm angles for a persuasive or personal essay, define rhetorical terms and get feedback on a paragraph. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes analyze a speech rhetorically and compare rhetorical frameworks.

What do I need to start using AI as a Writing and Rhetoric student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 Writing and Rhetoric use cases here draw on 14 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for Writing and Rhetoric coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for Writing and Rhetoric coursework at HWS?

Your Writing and Rhetoric professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Develop and test a style-transfer workflow" exists to get you through Writing and Rhetoric material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
