Philosophy AI questions, answered

How can Philosophy students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for Philosophy students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as summarize a kant reading and define philosophical terms through to project-scale work like design an experimental philosophy study. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a Philosophy major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For Philosophy that means summarize a kant reading, define philosophical terms and explain a thought experiment. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes reconstruct a philosopher's argument and compare competing theories.

What do I need to start using AI as a Philosophy student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 Philosophy use cases here draw on 13 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for Philosophy coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for Philosophy coursework at HWS?

Your Philosophy professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Design an experimental philosophy study" exists to get you through Philosophy material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
