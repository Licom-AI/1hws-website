Physics AI questions, answered

How can Physics students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for Physics students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as explain a physics concept and summarize a research paper abstract through to project-scale work like solve differential equations numerically. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a Physics major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For Physics that means explain a physics concept, summarize a research paper abstract and define entropy, flux, and eigenstate accessibly. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes set up a physics problem and build a thermodynamics exam review sheet.

What do I need to start using AI as a Physics student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 Physics use cases here draw on 12 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for Physics coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for Physics coursework at HWS?

Your Physics professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Solve differential equations numerically" exists to get you through Physics material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
