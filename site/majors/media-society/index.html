Media & Society AI questions, answered

How can Media & Society students use AI at HWS?

This page lists 20 practical AI use cases written specifically for Media & Society students at Hobart and William Smith Colleges — 10 easy, 6 medium, 4 hard by difficulty. They range from everyday coursework help such as summarize a mcluhan reading and define hegemony, framing, and agenda-setting through to project-scale work like scrape and analyze headlines. Each one comes with a starter prompt you can paste straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Which AI use cases should a Media & Society major try first?

Start with the 10 rated Easy — they need no setup and work on the first try. For Media & Society that means summarize a mcluhan reading, define hegemony, framing, and agenda-setting and brainstorm essay topics on streaming culture and surveillance. Once those feel routine, move up to the Medium tier, which includes compare two theorists and analyze a media-consumption survey dataset.

What do I need to start using AI as a Media & Society student?

A free account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is the whole list — no coding, no paid subscription, and nothing to install. The 20 Media & Society use cases here draw on 14 tutorial videos between them, and each card ships with a starter prompt already framed for Media & Society coursework, so the first thing you do is paste, not write.

Is it OK to use AI for Media & Society coursework at HWS?

Your Media & Society professor decides, and the answer changes from course to course and even between assignments — so read the syllabus before any of this touches graded work. Everything on this page is a study aid: a use case like "Scrape and analyze headlines" exists to get you through Media & Society material faster, not to produce something you submit as your own. If the syllabus is silent, ask before you use it.
